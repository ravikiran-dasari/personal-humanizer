## Post 4

Written fully by me: Partly / AI-assisted

🧠💥 Did Apple expose something the AI world isn’t ready to admit ?

Ever asked ChatGPT or Claude to solve a simple logic puzzle, and thought "This thing is very smart!"

As someone deep in exploring LLMs and RAG architectures, this latest research triggered my understanding perspective.

Apple's recent paper, "The Illusion of Thinking: Understanding the Strengths and Limitations of Reasoning Models via the Lens of Problem Complexity", drops a sharp reality check on how these AI models "think" or "they don't".
Apple tested all the big LLMs GPT, Claude, Gemini on classic reasoning puzzles. Tower of Hanoi, River crossing. The kind of stuff kids do in school.

🧩 Here's what happened:
▪️ Easy puzzles?  |  AI solved them quickly
▪️ Harder puzzles?  |  AI started to struggle
▪️ Complex puzzles?  |  AI accuracy collapsed
They still failed even after feeding them exact solving algorithms.

Here's what Apple mentioned:
One model used 100+ steps to solve "Tower of Hanoi" but couldn't manage 4 simple moves in a "River Crossing" puzzle. Why because the Hanoi puzzle existed in its training data. The river one didn't.

That's why Apple says: It's not reasoning it's just pattern matching.

⚠️ But researchers from OpenAI and Anthropic came back with their counterpoints:
✅ Models hit token limits, not cognitive boundaries.
✅ Some test puzzles were unsolvable by design.
✅ When asked to write algorithms instead of listing moves, models did fine.

So maybe it's not that models can't reason, it's that Apple might testing them wrong. Imagine asking a developer: "List every single keystroke you use to build this feature" instead of just letting them write the code.

📌 The real takeaway?
This research doesn't deny the AI revolution. But it forces us to be honest.
Right now, these models are brilliant pattern matching engines with excellent memory not the reasoning machines that we think they are.

🔗 I3C in Embedded Systems: Smarter Communication for Modern Devices

With embedded systems increasing in complexity and sensor numbers, a high-speed, low-power communication protocol becomes essential. That's when MIPI_Alliance announced I3C (Improved Inter-Integrated Circuit) in 2016 as the next-generation replacement for both I2C and SPI*.

🔹 What is I3C?
I3C is a advanced version of I2C serial communication protocol with same 2-wires, this provides high-speed data transfer of up to 33 Mbps, dynamic addressing, hot-join capability, and most importantly IBI (In-Band Interrupts).

⚡ Why Use I3C in Embedded Systems?
✅ In-Band Interrupt (IBI): Slave can alert the controller directly over the same bus lines (no extra IRQ pins needed)
✅ Up to 94% faster than I2C: Higher throughput improves system responsiveness.
✅ Reduced pin count: Only two lines for both data and interrupts, hence  simpler PCB design.
✅ Hot-Join & Dynamic Addressing: Add sensors on the fly without rebooting the system.
✅ Backward Compatible: Supports legacy I2C devices, easing the transition.

⚠️ Why I3C Isn’t Common yet in Embedded Systems?
Despite its many advantages, I3C is still not widely used in embedded systems for the following reasons.
🚫 Most MCUs don’t support I3C yet
📦 I2C and SPI are already well-known and widely used
🧠 Fewer sensors and chips available with I3C support
🔒 I3C was not fully open in the beginning (early licensing concerns)
🛠️ Many embedded projects don’t need advanced I3C features

It’s super interesting to see how I3C is shaping the future of embedded communication.

🚀 Microsoft’s Quantum Leap: A Game-Changer or Just Hype?

Quantum computing is one of the most exciting topic in tech, and Microsoft just made a claim that could change everything about QuantumProcessingUnit (QPU). 🔲

🔹 The Challenge with #QuantumComputers : 🌐
We need quantum computers to unlock new possibilities, like simulating nature more precisely, but building a useful one is really hard. The more qubits (quantum bits) we add, the more errors come in. That’s because qubits are super sensitive and easily disrupted.

🔹 Microsoft’s Big Idea: The Topological Qubit ⚛️
Instead of storing information in a single fragile spot, Microsoft’s qubit spreads it out using a new state of matter MajoranaQuasiparticles. In theory, this makes quantum computers more stable and scalable by reducing errors.

💡 Simply Put: Imagine writing a message on sand, but instead of a single spot that can be erased, the message is spread out across the entire surface. Even if a part gets disturbed, the message is still there.

🔹 The Controversy: 📢
Microsoft claims they not only created these qubits but can control them, which is a massive breakthrough! However, while their peer-reviewed paper suggests they’ve seen Majorana Quasiparticles, it doesn’t 100% prove it.

🔮 If this is true, it could be a major breakthrough in quantum computing. But until it’s confirmed, we can’t say for sure.

The Power of Brain-Computer Interfaces:

Brain-Computer Interfaces (BCI) create a direct communication link between the brain and external devices, enabling individuals to control them using brain signals. BCIs can be non-invasive, like EEG-based (electroencecephalogram) systems reading brain activity from the scalp, involving implanted devices for more precise readings. These interfaces have diverse applications, from assisting individuals with disabilities to enhancing human-computer interactions.

BCIs detect, analyze, and translate brainsignals into commands that control external devices, such as computers or prosthetic limbs. Ongoing researches of Neuralink FacebookRealityLabs CTRLLabs Emotiv Gtec companies aims to improve signal quality, user-friendliness, and expand applications in fields like medicine, rehabilitation, and everyday life.

The potential of BCIs is vast. They could revolutionize how individuals interact with technology, offering new possibilities for those with physical limitations. As the technology continues to advance, BCIs hold the promise of unlocking a future where the mind's power can reshape the world around us.
